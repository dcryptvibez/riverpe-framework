1. Market & Corridor Prioritization

Corridor-First Thinking

Think in corridors, not just markets. A corridor = origin market → destination market + payment method + use case.

Examples

  • Brazil → US (crypto off-ramp, iGaming payout)
  • Ivory Coast → France (remittance, ecommerce)
  • DRC → South Africa (B2B trade, payroll)
  • Brazil → Argentina (cross-border ecommerce)

Recommendation: First 90 days — operate through licensed local PSP partners. Riverpe = technology/aggregation layer. Direct licensing = Year 2 for high-volume markets.

High-Risk Industry Screening Questions

For crypto, iGaming, and forex PSP selection:

  1. Does the PSP explicitly accept these industries?
  2. Do their banking partners allow these transactions?
  3. What risk controls exist for high-risk flows?
  4. Have they experienced account freezes or compliance actions?
  5. What transaction limits apply to high-risk merchants?
  6. What's their dispute/chargeback handling capability?
Critical: Never hide the crypto use case from PSP partners. Hidden crypto flows surfacing during a regulatory audit will destroy the partnership.

How Regulation Affects Partner Selection

  • Without proper licensing → automatically disqualified
  • History of regulatory sanctions → high-risk, low-volume pilot only
  • Compliance infrastructure → determines scaling ceiling
  • Risk appetite → determines which use cases they support
  • Banking relationship stability → determines operational continuity

3. Mapping the Payment Ecosystem

The Layer Model

Layer 1: Banks

Settlement banks, commercial banks, fintech-friendly banks, FX providers

Layer 2: Mobile Money & Wallets

Mobile operators (M-Pesa, Airtel, MTN, Orange, Wave), digital wallets, alternative payment methods

Layer 3: PSPs & Aggregators

Payment gateways, local processors, acquirers, API providers

Market-Specific Ecosystems

🇨🇮 Ivory Coast

  • Mobile money: Orange Money (~45%), MTN MoMo (~30%), Wave (~20%), Free Money (~5%)
  • Banks: SGBCI, BICICI, Ecobank, NSIA Banke, Coris Bank
  • PSPs: Flutterwave, FedaPay, CinetPay, ReliaMoney
  • Card penetration ~5% — mobile money dominates. CFA franc zone simplifies WAEMU payments.

🇧🇷 Brazil

  • PIX: 60%+ of all digital payments — instantaneous, 24/7, zero consumer cost
  • Boleto Bancário: ~20% of e-commerce (cash-based, 3-day settlement)
  • PSPs: PagSeguro, Mercado Pago, StoneCo, EBANX, dLocal, Cielo, Rede
  • Open Banking rolling out under BACEN. NuBank, Inter, C6 Bank reshaping banking.

🇨🇩 Congo (DRC)

  • Cash: ~90% of all transactions
  • Mobile money: Airtel Money (largest), Orange Money, M-Pesa (limited)
  • Banks: Rawbank, Equity BCDC, TMB, FBNBank
  • Very limited PSP infrastructure — this is where Riverpe creates value. Dual-currency (USD + CDF).

Partner Discovery Process

  1. Industry Databases: The Paypers, CB Insights, Fintech Maps, MRC member lists
  2. Fintech Associations: Africa FinTech Network, ABFintech, PASA
  3. Regulatory Directories: Central Bank licensed entity registers
  4. Competitor Analysis: Map who Flutterwave, Paystack, dLocal, Chipper Cash use — if they process real volume through a PSP, that PSP is proven
  5. Local Consultants: $500–2,000 for structured market mapping per market
  6. Conferences: Africa Fintech Summit, Money20/20, Seamless Africa, DEVCON

Corridor PSP Structure

Primary PSP — Best performance, main volume (70–80% of traffic)

Backup PSP — Failover, risk reduction (15–25% of traffic, scales up if primary degrades)

Future PSP — Pricing negotiation leverage, integrated but low volume, ready to activate

Industry-Specific PSP Identification

🏦 Crypto

  • Filter by explicit crypto-friendly policy — ask directly, verify with references
  • Check correspondent bank is also crypto-tolerant
  • Verify travel rule and source-of-funds compliance
  • Look for: Yellow Card, Bundle, Valr (Africa); Bitso, Mercado Bitcoin (Brazil)

🎰 iGaming

  • Requires explicit risk appetite confirmation
  • Verify chargeback/dispute handling
  • Check settlement speed — gaming operators need fast fund access

📈 Forex

  • Handle frequent, high-value, cross-border transactions
  • Check multi-currency account or FX capabilities
  • Verify licensing covers forex-related processing

5. Corridor Launch Strategy — 90-Day Plan

Principle: Launch narrow, learn fast, expand methodically. One primary corridor. Not five markets simultaneously.
Phase 1
Days 1–30
Market Discovery

Complete market scoring with actual data. 10+ customer discovery calls. Regulatory feasibility with local legal advisors. Map payment ecosystem. Identify 20–30 PSP candidates per market.

Deliverable: Market scorecard, demand validation, ecosystem map, PSP longlist. Decision: select first corridor.

Phase 2
Days 30–45
Partner Selection

Score top 10 PSPs using 100-point scorecard. Shortlist 5–10. Outreach via warm intros. 8–10 discovery calls. Full due diligence on top 4–6. Negotiate commercial terms.

Deliverable: Signed agreements with primary + backup PSPs.

Phase 3
Days 45–75
Integration

Build PSP abstraction layer from Day 1. API integration with sandbox. Payment flow implementation. Error handling, retry logic, alerting. Integration + security testing. Settlement testing. Compliance approval.

Key: PSP abstraction layer is non-negotiable. Adding new PSPs must be a config change, not a rewrite.

Phase 4
Days 75–90
Pilot & Launch

3–5 pilot customers per corridor. $1K–$10K controlled volume. Monitor every transaction. Collect feedback. Manual reconciliation. If pilot succeeds → open for general onboarding.

Success: >95% success rate, on-time settlement, zero compliance incidents. Deliverable: One live corridor with metrics dashboard. Begin scoping second corridor.

Payment Failure Management

Failure TypeResponseAction
Technical (PSP downtime)Auto-retry on backupAlert PSP, log incident
Compliance (AML block)Route to complianceNo auto-retry
Liquidity (insufficient funds)Retry on backupAlert PSP for replenishment
Customer (wrong details)Notify customerNo retry
Escalation: Failure rate >5% → immediate review. >10% → corridor hold. Repeated failures from same PSP → volume shift.

6. Payment Supply Network Management

Principle: After launch, the network becomes a data business. The companies that win combine local partnerships, compliance discipline, payment reliability, and intelligent routing.

Failure ReasonsMostly customerMostly PSP-sideTechnical review
Customer Complaints<0.5%>2%Joint investigation
API Uptime>99.5%<98%SLA enforcement

Smart Payment Routing

Cost-based routing: Lowest cost PSP for each payment type, amount, corridor

Reliability-based routing: Highest success rate for that transaction type

Load balancing: Distribute volume to maintain leverage + avoid single points of failure

Latency-based routing: Fastest settlement for time-sensitive payments

The Data Flywheel

Better success rates → more customers → more volume → stronger PSP relationships → better pricing → more customers → repeat

Transaction Data as Competitive Advantage

  1. Pricing intelligence: True cost of every payment type in every corridor
  2. Reliability benchmarks: Quantified PSP performance by payment type
  3. Demand prediction: Volume trends reveal growth, seasonality, emerging demand
  4. Risk modeling: More data = better models = lower costs
  5. Customer insight: Payment behavior reveals new product opportunities

Why These Numbers

  • Q1 is about proving one corridor works, not scaling. Most corridors take 2–4 weeks of customer discovery + 4–8 weeks of integration. Getting 1 corridor live in 90 days is a realistic win.
  • $25–100K monthly volume in Q1 is honest for pilot customers. If it's higher, great — but don't plan around it.
  • Revenue of $1–4K/month in Q1 covers 1–2 people's time, not the company's burn. That's fine — this is a supply-side investment.
  • Success rate starts at >90% because pilot transactions will surface edge cases. >95% by Q2 is realistic after fixing those.
  • Cost reduction of -5% to -10% is achievable through volume renegotiation. -20% requires 12+ months of leverage.

8. Final Perspective

My first objective would not be building the largest PSP network. It would be building the most reliable payment network.

The Winning Strategy

Find demand → Validate regulation → Map ecosystem → Select quality PSPs → Launch one corridor → Optimize through data → Expand regionally

Key Principles

  • Corridor-first thinking — think in routes, not markets
  • Reliability over scale — one working corridor beats five broken ones
  • Data compounds — every transaction makes the network smarter
  • Transparency with partners — never hide the use case, especially crypto
  • Redundancy is non-negotiable — 2+ PSPs per corridor, always
  • Depth before breadth — prove one corridor, then replicate
